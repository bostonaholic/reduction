Espresso Brownies

By the Reduction test kitchen · 1 hour · makes 16 squares

A single shot of espresso will not make these brownies taste like coffee. What it does is turn the cocoa up: darker, rounder, more like chocolate than chocolate manages on its own. This is the fudgy end of the brownie spectrum — a shiny, papery crust over a center that bends before it breaks.

The method matters more than the shopping list. Melting the butter instead of creaming it is what keeps the crumb dense, and beating the eggs in until the batter turns glossy is what builds that crust. Neither takes skill; both take believing the instructions.

Ingredients

  • 4 oz (115 g) unsalted butter
  • 1 cup (200 g) sugar
  • 1/4 tsp. vanilla extract
  • 1 shot fresh brewed espresso
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/3 cup cocoa powder
  • 1/4 tsp. baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp. table salt

Method

  1. Butter and flour an 8x8-inch pan. Chase the flour into the corners and knock out the excess — a bare patch is where the first brownie will refuse to leave.
  2. Preheat oven to 350 degrees F. Give it the full ten minutes; a brownie that starts in a cold oven bakes flat and never grows its crust.
  3. Melt the unsalted butter in a small saucepan over low heat, pulling it off the moment the last piece disappears. Browned butter is lovely elsewhere; here it fights the espresso.
  4. Mix the melted butter with the sugar, vanilla extract, and espresso. The sugar will not dissolve completely and does not need to — the undissolved grains are half the secret of the crackled top.
  5. Mix in the eggs until the batter is glossy. This is the one place to be thorough: a full minute of stirring, until the batter turns from grainy to shining and ribbons off the spoon.
  6. Fold in the all-purpose flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, and table salt. Stop at the last streak of flour; every stroke past that point trades fudge for cake.
  7. Bake at 350 degrees F for 30 to 40 minutes, until a toothpick at the center comes out with moist crumbs but no wet batter. Cool completely in the pan before cutting — warm brownies cut like mud.

Notes

Dutch-process or natural cocoa both work; the espresso flatters either. If you have no machine, a scant quarter cup of strong moka pot coffee does the job. These keep for three days in a tin, improving on the second day in the way fudgy things do.
